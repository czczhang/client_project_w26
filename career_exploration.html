Explore Possibilities

Use reflection, research, and small experiments to learn what fits you best.

Career exploration is not about choosing one perfect path immediately. It is about gathering enough information to make stronger decisions over time.

Questions to ask yourself

  • What topics, problems, or communities interest me most?
  • What kind of work environment helps me do my best work?
  • Which skills do I want to deepen this year?
  • What type of impact feels meaningful to me?

Ways to explore careers

  • Attend employer events and career fairs.
  • Talk with alumni or professionals in roles you want to understand better.
  • Review job descriptions to spot patterns in tools and responsibilities.
  • Try projects, internships, research, or volunteer work that test your interests.

Common career paths for UMSI students

These are not the only options, but they are useful starting points for comparing interests and skills.

User Experience

Design or research roles focused on understanding users and improving products or services.

Data and Analytics

Roles that involve working with data, trends, dashboards, and decision support.

Product and Strategy

Work centered on product planning, cross-functional collaboration, and problem solving.

Libraries and Information Services

Careers focused on information organization, access, research support, and community service.

Consulting and Client Work

Projects that require communication, analysis, and adapting solutions to different stakeholders.

Emerging Intersections

Many students combine interests across UX, data, policy, accessibility, health, and public service.

Simple exploration plan

  1. Choose one field or role you want to understand better this week.
  2. Read two or three job descriptions and note repeated skills or tools.
  3. Talk with one person or attend one event related to that path.
  4. Write down what sounds energizing, confusing, or worth testing next.

A helpful reminder

Exploration works best when you stay curious. You do not need to have your full path figured out now; you only need the next good question and one concrete step forward.
